# ChartQuest audio direction — founder audition

**Status:** isolated pad-free music audition. Exploration Core + Snare, Trade Decision Point, Guardian Collision, and all four new Review choices are founder-locked. This is not a game integration or a release candidate.

Open [the audition page](chartquest-audio-audition.html) in a modern browser, reload it if already open, and press **Enable audio**. The **Approved audio · Review choices** panel holds the three locked state picks and opens Review on **Continue Exploration** (take 8). In the Review take list, all four founder-approved choices remain available: **Continue Exploration** (8), **Forward Page** (9), **Chamber Steps** (10), and **Quiet Review** (11). The page's starting Review choice does not establish the sole in-game Review cue. Earlier slow Review experiments remain below the same state. Every music take is free of sustained pads and music reverb. Each category remembers its selected take until reload. Headphones at a comfortable volume are recommended. Every sound is synthesized with Web Audio; the page loads no samples, fonts, scripts, or network resources.

## Musical direction

Warm electronic adventure built from short keyboard phrases, low pulses, heartbeat motifs, and tactile percussion. Sustained chord pads and the music-room return have been removed from **all** music takes. The sound-effect room remains, except the Target effect's former pad has also been removed. The mix prioritizes clear attacks and space on mobile speakers without orchestral swells, rap, or slot-machine chimes.

Founder confirmed that removing the pad improved Compass, then explored key replies, ghost percussion, and a Balanced mix. Later feedback identified notes that seemed disconnected from the rhythm. Guardian's later revisions sounded worse to the founder, who explicitly locked the original **Collision**. The founder then locked Exploration **Core + Snare**. For Trade, the founder prefers the original **Decision Point** heartbeat and rhythm, but disliked the high note every other bar. The selected take replaces that chime with a brief, quiet filtered drum ghost; the heartbeat, bass, chord stabs, and 108 BPM tempo remain. After auditioning that change, the founder locked **Decision Point** as the Trade choice. The selected Exploration and Guardian takes have not been resynthesized in these selection passes.

## Research basis and testable question

There is no supported claim here that classical music, a specific tempo, or these prototypes will increase ChartQuest player retention. The word **retention** needs two separate measures: return to the game later and recall/comprehension of what the player reviewed. These studies are directional evidence, not measurements of ChartQuest:

- In an [experiment with 81 college students](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01902/full), instrumental background music during text learning did not improve recall, and comprehension was worse for the lowest working-memory group. This supports a quiet comparison for text-heavy Review moments.
- A [90-person reading experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38646111/) found that music with lyrics reduced reading comprehension compared with no music. The new samples contain no lyrics.
- A [randomized educational-game study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0023969025000931) found higher achievement with music but also higher cognitive load in its seventh-grade sample. It did not establish a universal music style or measure long-term game return.
- A [game soundtrack experiment](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/15213269.2018.1507827) found an effect on enjoyment through positive emotions in short play sessions. Enjoyment is relevant but is not a measured day-later return rate.

**Inference for the audition:** continuity may avoid an awkward mood drop, while low-density instrumental options or silence may leave more room for reading. The first test should compare Review completion, a short recall check, music mute rate, and next-session return across the candidate cues with the same Review content and volume settings. No retention outcome is claimed for these samples.

## Integration contract for a later slice

The game should own **one** audio director. Its public surface can stay small: `enableFromUserGesture()`, `setScene(scene)`, `playCue(cue)`, `setMuted(boolean)`, and `setNarrationActive(boolean)`. The game maps its own states and events to these abstract calls. The director owns one music transport, one current scene bus, one effect bus, an effect-room return, and one final master gain. Every path reaches that master.

- **Activation:** create or resume the audio context only after a player gesture. The page's Enable audio button demonstrates this. The game should retain its existing preference authority; this prototype stores nothing.
- **Scene change:** retire the previous scene bus over about 450 ms while fading in the next over about 550 ms. Stop scheduling notes for the old scene immediately. A rapid second change retires the first transition too; no stacked music loops persist. If Review uses **Continue Exploration**, keep the existing Core + Snare transport running instead of restarting it.
- **Master mute:** gate the final output for music, effects, and active tails; stop music scheduling while muted. Unmuting starts only the selected scene. No cue may bypass the master. On page hide, stop the transport and silence output.
- **Narration:** `setNarrationActive(true)` ramps the music bus to roughly 24% over 220 ms, then restores it when narration ends. Effects remain separately controlled. The game must decide whether a particular spoken scene also suppresses specific cues.
- **Effect focus:** the audition page briefly reduces music to 18% for an effect preview and restores it after the cue. Its effect-level slider is audition-only. The Target effect's pad was removed with the global pad removal; a separate effect-design pass remains. A game integration should tune relative loudness by event priority and leave player volume preference in the existing system.
- **Cue limits:** movement should be rate-limited in gameplay; repetitive event cues need a concurrency/priority budget. A high-priority result or Guardian hit can supersede a low-priority movement cue. The audition page intentionally allows repeated taps for comparison.
- **Compatibility:** prefer existing game mute and accessibility settings when integrated. This prototype must not be copied over the game's current audio ownership, Boss cinematic sync, or saved `cq_music` setting without a scoped review.

## Recommended first integration slice

Wire **only the founder-locked Exploration loop** to one non-critical traversal state behind a local opt-in flag after a scoped integration decision. Route it through the game's existing music/mute authority. Verify gesture startup, mute while a note sounds, background/foreground return, scene exit, and phone-speaker balance. Add other music states and redesigned effects only after that slice is accepted. Approval of the four Review sounds does not select runtime conditions or approve game integration.

## Review limits

The browser may change loudness and timbre by device. The prototype has no measured loudness target, physical-device listening result, gameplay event timing, retention experiment, or production QA claim. Founder listening locked Exploration Core + Snare, the edited original Trade Decision Point, Guardian Collision, and Review takes 8–11. Effect quality remains an open creative issue for a later pass. Runtime selection among approved Review choices and relative levels remain to be defined before implementation.
